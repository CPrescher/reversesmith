Subject: Permission to use EPSR26 silica workshop files in an academic software benchmark

Dear Dr Hughes and Dr Bowron,

We are preparing an academic paper for the *Journal of Applied
Crystallography* describing `rsmith`, an open-source reverse Monte Carlo and
hybrid RMC program for total-scattering refinement.

We would like to reproduce the amorphous-silica exercise distributed with the
EPSR26 Workshop 2024 materials. Our first aim is a fair validation: run the
official EPSR26 workflow with its original neutron and X-ray inputs, reference
potential, density, and analysis settings, and show whether `rsmith` reproduces
the resulting fitted totals and structural distributions. We would then compare
EPSR with pair-potential and machine-learning-potential variants of `rsmith` at
matched agreement with the experimental data.

The EPSR download conditions state that raw and demonstration files require
express permission for use in publicity or publication. Could you please
confirm whether we may:

1. use the EPSR26 Workshop 2024 amorphous-silica input and demonstration files
   for this non-commercial academic study;
2. publish plots and numerical metrics derived from those files and from EPSR26
   output;
3. archive the original benchmark inputs with the paper, for example on Zenodo;
   or, if redistribution is not permitted, publish only checksums, conversion
   scripts, derived curves, and instructions that require readers to download
   EPSR from the official ISIS page;
4. quote the relevant EPSR settings and small input excerpts needed to make the
   comparison reproducible.

The legacy links currently listed for `EPSR26-Workshop-2024.zip` and
`EPSR26distribution-2022-06-06.zip` return HTTP 404 following the ISIS website
migration. Could you also provide their current official download locations or
send us copies for local execution testing?

We will cite the EPSR manual and the publications you recommend, clearly label
EPSR26 as the reference implementation, report all replicate runs rather than a
selected best result, and share the comparison protocol with you if useful.
Please also let us know your preferred acknowledgement and citation wording.
